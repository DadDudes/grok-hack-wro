EduBotGames · brand exploration · round 1

Robo, games
and a voice

Five logo systems, four Robos, five palettes, three type pairs. The pick becomes repo assets, the README style section, the portal tokens and the promo video kit.

From your spec

  • Kids 6-12 design a learning game by voice, agents build it, Robo plays along. Parents see progress.
  • Playful and bright for kids, clean enough that parents trust it. No ads look.
  • Every screen reads in 2 seconds in a 30-45 s vertical video. Robo on every screen.

Research in short

  • Violet and blue are the category default: Kahoot, Quizlet, SplashLearn, Scratch, Lingokids.
  • Brief purple #6C4DF6 is dE 9 from Quizlet blue. Teal and berry are open (dE 50+).
  • Robot heads are stock icons, so the mark needs a game or voice twist.

  • Logo A-E, Robo R1-R4, palette P1-P4, type T1-T3. Hybrids welcome ("A with R3").

A Robo face

Collision risk High
Mark
App icon · 64 / 32 / 16

The logo is Robo. One face from the app icon to the hint bubble to the last frame of the video.

Why it can work

Your spec wants Robo on every screen so the video reads as one story. Logo and mascot merge, nothing extra to learn.

Watch out

Robot heads are stock icons (Material smart_toy, many AI assistants). It stays ours only through the ear bolts, the screen face and the colour.

In the product

The screen face swaps expressions: thinking dots while agents build, happy eyes on a correct answer. The icon itself can animate.

B Pad bot

Collision risk Med
Mark
App icon · 64 / 32 / 16

A game controller with a face. Game and bot in one shape; the antenna turns a controller into a teammate.

Why it can work

Games are the hook for kids 6-12. A controller reads at 32 px and is not an owl, a book or a lightbulb like most edu marks.

Watch out

Controller icons are everywhere in gaming. Some parents may read it as gaming first, learning second.

In the product

Eyes are the thumbsticks. On the game screen the two eyes become the two players and the antenna ball is Robo.

C Say and play

Collision risk Med
Mark
App icon · 64 / 32 / 16

A speech bubble with an antenna and a play button. The product in one sign: you say it, a bot builds it, you play it.

Why it can work

Voice is the step nobody else has: the kid designs the game by talking. The play triangle works even for kids who cannot read yet.

Watch out

Bubble plus play also reads as video chat or podcasts. No face, so Robo has to carry the warmth on his own.

In the product

The bubble is the mic button on screen 2. The triangle turns into a mic while the kid speaks and back into play when the game is ready.

D Pixel bot

Collision risk Med
Mark
App icon · 64 / 32 / 16

Robo drawn as an 8-bit sprite. It says "this is a game" with no extra symbol and stays sharp at 16 px.

Why it can work

Pixels are the language of the games kids already build (Minecraft, Roblox). Frame animation in Phaser is cheap.

Watch out

Retro pixel art is popular with indie games. Feels nostalgic to parents more than fresh to kids.

In the product

The same pixel grid makes loading bars, the team score, sprites in the templates and transitions in the video.

E Cartridge bot

Collision risk Low
Mark
App icon · 64 / 32 / 16

A game cartridge with Robo on the label. Every kid's idea becomes their own cartridge.

Why it can work

The cartridge is the product: a game made just for you. No edu competitor owns this shape.

Watch out

Most detailed of the five: the ridges vanish at 16 px. Kids know Switch cards more than big cartridges.

In the product

The game library is a shelf of cartridges with the kid's titles ("Dinos in Space"). Strong for the share card.

Four Robos, one teammate

R1Screen Robo
Round head, screen face, one antenna.
Where it comes fromStraight from your design brief. Pairs with A, works with every direction.
ReadMost expressive: the screen shows any face, loading dots or a star. Easy to pose.
R2Pad Robo
The head is a game controller.
Where it comes fromThe body of direction B. Says "game" before anything else.
ReadFewer expressions: the eyes are fixed buttons, so poses rely on arms.
R3Pixel Robo
An 8-bit sprite.
Where it comes fromThe grid of direction D. Native inside Phaser.
ReadCheap to animate frame by frame. Less cuddly for a 6 year old.
R4Bubble Robo
A floating speech bubble with a face.
Where it comes fromThe bubble of direction C. Floats next to hints.
ReadNo legs, so he hovers. Feels like a voice more than a toy.
